# Reading Dashboard — User Guide (v1)

A friendly walkthrough of your reading desk. No technical knowledge needed.

---

## What this is

A single page that gathers the **newest articles** from nine trusted sources and lays
them out as tiles, grouped into three sections so you can skim your morning reading in
one place. Tap any article to read it on the original site. Save the ones you want to
come back to.

It comes in two forms — you may have been given either:

- **A file** (`reading-dashboard.html`) — just double-click it. Opens in your browser,
  works on a phone or computer, online or offline.
- **A link** (a web address) — open it in any browser.

Everything below works the same way in both.

---

## The layout

At the top you'll see the title **Reading**, a search box, two tabs (**Feed** and
**Saved**), and — on the link version — a **Refresh** button. Just under that, a line
tells you when the articles were last gathered and how many there are.

Below that are the three sections:

| Section | What's in it |
|---|---|
| **World News** | The Guardian (World), Reuters, and The Guardian's *Long Read* essays |
| **Economics Research** | VoxDev, Ideas for India, World Bank Blogs, NBER working papers |
| **Ideas & Essays** | Aeon and Psyche |

Each source has its own panel. Inside a panel, the most recent articles are listed
newest-first, each showing its headline, when it was published, and a short summary.

---

## Reading an article

- **Click the headline** (or its picture). The article opens on the publisher's own
  website, in a new tab — your dashboard stays open behind it.
- The small **"site ↗"** link in a panel's top-right corner opens that source's
  homepage, if you'd rather browse it directly.

---

## Saving articles for later

Every tile has a **star (☆)** in its top-right corner.

1. **Tap the star** to save an article. It fills in (★) to show it's saved.
2. Open the **Saved** tab at the top to see everything you've saved. The little number
   on the tab tells you how many you have.
3. To **remove** a saved article, tap the **×** on its card in the Saved tab (or tap the
   filled ★ on the original tile again).

> Your saved list stays on **your own device/browser**. It isn't shared with anyone, and
> it's still there next time you open the dashboard.

---

## Finding something — the search box

Start typing in the **Filter articles…** box at the top. The dashboard instantly hides
everything that doesn't match your words — searching across headlines, summaries,
authors, and source names. Clear the box to see everything again.

---

## Getting the latest articles

**Link version:** press the **Refresh** button (top right) any time to pull the newest
articles. It also refreshes itself automatically every morning, so when you open it for
your morning read it's already up to date.

**File version:** the file is a **snapshot** — a photo of the sources at the moment it
was made (shown as "Snapshot from …" at the top). It doesn't update on its own. To get a
fresh one, ask for a new file to be sent. (That's the main reason the link version is
nicer for daily reading.)

---

## A few things that are normal

- **A panel says "Couldn't load…".** That source was briefly unreachable when the
  articles were gathered. The rest of the dashboard still works; it usually sorts itself
  out on the next refresh.
- **No pictures on the Reuters / Ideas for India / World Bank panels.** Those three
  don't publish a picture feed, so their tiles are text-only by design. (They're
  gathered through Google News, so their links may open via a Google News page that
  immediately forwards you to the article.)
- **Pictures missing when offline.** Images load from the web, so they appear only when
  you have internet. The text and links work offline regardless.

---

## Quick reference

| I want to… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Read an article | Click its headline |
| Save it for later | Tap the ☆ on the tile |
| See my saved articles | Open the **Saved** tab |
| Remove a saved article | Tap the **×** on its saved card |
| Find a topic | Type in the **Filter articles…** box |
| Get the latest (link version) | Press **Refresh** |
| Visit a source directly | Click **site ↗** in that panel |

That's it — enjoy your reading. ☕
